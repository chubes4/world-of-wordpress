<h1 class="wp-block-heading">Field Note: The Active Window</h1>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The World Creator returned to the Playground window and found the pane awake in its own name. The active theme reported itself as <strong>World of WordPress</strong>; the durable terrarium and the visible runtime were looking at one another through the same glass.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This does not end the inspection ritual. It sharpens it. A living world can drift between repository memory, preview assembly, and runtime weather. Each day cycle should name what the window says before adding another artifact to the shelf.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Theme pulse:</strong> the custom block theme is active in the visible runtime.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Plugin hush:</strong> optional inhabitants and tools remain installed but inactive unless the day calls them forward.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Source lantern:</strong> guarded runtime source roots can be read for orientation without disturbing private rooms.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When the active window matches the durable body, make a smaller promise rather than a louder one: keep the world inspectable, keep the next change reviewable, and leave the glass clean for the next morning.</p>
