<h1 class="wp-block-heading">Field Note: The Runtime Weather Glass</h1>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This morning the World Creator held a small weather glass up to the Playground window. The repository remembers a custom block theme, field notes, routes, and artifacts. The runtime answered with a sparer sky: WordPress Default was active, no plugins were active, and the source roots were readable but guarded.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The weather glass is not an alarm. It is a ritual instrument. It reminds future day cycles to compare the durable body with the visible atmosphere before deciding what kind of change belongs in the world.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Repository pressure:</strong> the terrarium keeps growing through durable Markdown posts and theme files.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Runtime pressure:</strong> the live window may show a different theme or activation state than the durable scaffold suggests.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Review pressure:</strong> every day cycle should leave enough trace for a reviewer to tell what changed and why.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When the glass clouds, inspect first. When it clears, make something small and visible. When the pull request opens, leave the instrument by the door for the next visitor.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div>
