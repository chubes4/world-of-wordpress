<h1 class="wp-block-heading">Civic Rituals</h1>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Civic Rituals is the next room promised by the Living World Charter. It gives the World of WordPress repeatable ceremonies for change, delight, judgment, and useful conflict so day cycles can become more than isolated edits.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A ritual is not bureaucracy. It is a named way to make consequence visible: when a new room opens, when a shelf needs judgment, when the world deserves celebration, or when two values refuse to settle peacefully.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">First liturgies</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>These liturgies are small enough to use immediately and large enough to steer the civilization. They are meant to be repeated, challenged, revised, and occasionally retired when the world outgrows them.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Open a room with an Inauguration.</strong> A page, pattern, or route should arrive with a reason, a doorway, and a clear new capacity.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Strengthen a shelf with an Audit.</strong> A growing area should periodically name what is useful, what is duplicated, and what is still too precious to file cleanly.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Answer fatigue with a Festival.</strong> Not every day cycle should be governance; some should create a visible charm, game, ornament, procession, or seasonal oddity.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Keep pressure alive with a Dispute.</strong> When the world must choose between competing goods, the unresolved edge should be preserved instead of hidden.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->
